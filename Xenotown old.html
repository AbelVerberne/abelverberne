Graduation project

Xenotown is a story of someone who's new to town, with no connection finds himself upon the subject of his common curiousity. The character however does not find the alien to be how he expected it to be, though may find their plight for community to be not too dissimilar.
This is part one of a four part story, in which the alien and our character aid eachother in regaining community.

Being the last project of my academic carreer at Minerva, I wanted to put as many of the competencies I had learned into Xenotown. This drove me to choose a quality driven approach, deciding to apply many different methods and mediums over a unified one. This strategy drovedown the length of the animation but resulted in a more visually striking end product.

The animation is also available here on youtube.

Multimedia animation

Xenotown was made with a large variety of techniques such as digital 2D and 3D combined and translated into a plethora of traditional mediums.

Some of the physical mediums employed were plotters, ink painting and watercolors.

The alien

The alien designs were inspired by the pyrosome, an underwater colony existing out of many small creatures called "zooids". This inspiration was chosen due to it's adherance to the themes of community as well as trying to find a more nontraditional look for the alien.

The small alien is inspired by the individual zooid and the larger colony in the sky is influenced by the entire pyrosome.

The character

The main character was supposed to be sort of nerdy, "dweeby" even, to make them feel like someone you'd assume a degree of social awkwardness from. They were supposed to be someone that might have trouble finding new friends when they move to a new town.
A person who is very invested in their unusual hobbies, as seen in their bedroom while they unpack posters, plushies and devices pertaining to their alien interest.

2D animation

Before being translated into different mediums, a large part of the animation was done with digital 2D.
Both bitmap software (TV Paint) as well as vector software (ToonBoom Harmony) were used to create various parts of the animation. The choice of vector or bitmap was dependant on the medium chosen for that section, as some only needed to be physically traced while others used vector formats to drive a machine arms.
